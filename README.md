## 研究問題 Research Questions

1. 權威的定義為何？Kelsen 如何理解「計畫重建」與「直接分析」兩種進路之異同？  
   *What is authority? How does Kelsen relate "planned reconstruction" to direct conceptual analysis?*

2. Kelsen 的法律體系是否具有權威？其規範架構能否支撐一種法律權威概念的存在？  
   *Does Kelsen's legal system possess authority? Can his normative architecture sustain such a concept?*

3. Kelsen 的充權規範體系能否支持「弱意義」而非「強閱讀」的權威概念？  
   *Can Kelsen's empowerment norms support a "weak" rather than "strong" reading of authority?*

## What's Visualised

The site translates six chapters of legal philosophy into seven interactive sections, presented bilingually in Traditional Chinese and English.

**1 — Hero / 頁首**  
Thesis title, abstract, and authorship information in bilingual format. An orienting prose summary of the central argument.

**2 — Research Questions / 問題意識**  
The three core research questions presented as interactive cards, making the thesis's analytical agenda legible at a glance.

**3 — Kelsen's Three Intellectual Periods / 思想三分期**  
An annotated timeline tracing Kelsen's development from the Critical-Constructive Period (c. 1911–21) through the Classical Period (c. 1921–60) to the Skeptical Period (c. 1960–73). Each period includes key works, theoretical shifts, and their implications for the authority question.

**4 — Four Classifications of Authority / 權威四重分類**  
An interactive grid presenting the four principal ways of classifying authority: Divine/Secular, Sociological-Functional, De jure/De facto, and Raz's distinction between "being in authority" and "being an authority."

**5 — Four Theorists / 思想家比較**  
A tabbed comparison panel covering the four key figures in the thesis — Hobbes, Finnis, Raz, and Kelsen — each presenting their core claim, key difficulty or contribution, and relevance to Kelsen's framework.

**6 — The Stufenbau / 規範階層論**  
Kelsen's hierarchical structure of legal norms rendered as an expandable five-tier pyramid: Grundnorm → Constitution → Statutes → Regulations → Individual Acts. Each tier expands on click with bilingual explanations of its role in the chain of validity.

**7 — Late Kelsen: Norm Functions, Validity Types, and Conclusions**  
The four functions of norms in late Kelsen (Commanding, Derogating, Permitting, Empowering); the three types of legal validity (Systemic, Factual, Axiological); and the thesis's four conclusions, with scroll-triggered reveal animations.

## Core Argument

The thesis proposes a "weak reading" of authority in Kelsen — a position between two extremes:

| Position | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Sceptical view** | Kelsen has no authority concept; his system is merely a chain of empowerment norms with no genuine authoriser |
| **Strong reading** | Kelsen's system can support a full, traditional concept of legitimate authority (as in Raz or Hobbes) |
| **Thesis's position** | Kelsen's empowerment norms constitute a depersonalised, normative "weak authority" — sufficient to operate the legal system, but not reducible to traditional legitimate authority |

The central evidence is the role of **充權規範 (Empowerment Norms)** in the Stufenbau: norms that confer upon persons the legal power to create further norms. This constitutes an "authoriser-relationship" — weak, depersonalised, and normative — that can be extracted from Kelsen's architecture without distorting it.

## Intellectual Context

This thesis is situated at the intersection of three fields:

- **Legal positivism** — the tradition running from Bentham and Austin through Kelsen and Hart
- **Philosophy of authority** — the analytical debate from Raz's *The Authority of Law* (1979) and *Authority* (1990)
- **Neo-Kantianism** — the methodological background of Kelsen's early work, especially the influence of Heinrich Rickert and Emil Lask

The primary secondary literature includes Stanley Paulson's work on Kelsen's authority (the "weak reading" and "sceptical" positions), which the thesis engages with directly as its main interlocutor.
